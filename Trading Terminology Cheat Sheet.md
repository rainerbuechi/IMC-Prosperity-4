# IMC Trading Cheat sheet stole

| Algorithmic Trading | Algorithmic trading automates decision-making and trade execution in financial markets using complex mathematical models |
| --- | --- |
| Market Making | Market makers play an essential role in financial markets by providing buy and sell quotations and ensuring liquidity. They profit from the bid-ask spread (difference between the prices), enabling efficient trading for investors. |
| Locational arbitrage | Market inefficiency means an asset's price doesn't accurately reflect its true value, creating profit opportunities. Arbitrage takes advantage of these inefficiencies by exploiting price differences in the **same** security. |
| Statistical arbitrage | Statistical arbitrage is market neutral because they involve opening both a long position and short position simultaneously to take advantage of inefficient pricing in **correlated** securities. |
| Scalping | Trading strategy that focuses on profiting from minor price changes within a brief period. This strategy involves executing many trades daily to compound small profits into larger gains (What Day traders hope to do) |
| ETF  | Exchange-traded fund is a basket of securities that trades on an exchange, just like a stock. ETFs offer low expense ratios and fewer brokerage commissions than buying stocks individually. |
| Option | An option is a type of financial instrument that's tied to an underlying security. Options give their buyers the right, but not the obligation, to purchase or sell the asset at a specified price. |
| Short Position | A short position involves selling a security with the aim to buy it back later at a lower price.  Betting that a security will decrease in value. An investor with a short position has sold shares but doesn't possess them yet, obligated to buy them when the trade settles. |
| Long Position | A long position means an investor has bought and owns shares of stock. This investor has paid for the shares in full, makes money if the shares rise in value and are then sold. |
| Alpha | Alpha represents the active return on an investment compared to a market index or benchmark, highlighting the skill of a portfolio manager in adding value beyond standard market returns. |
| Beta | Beta is an indicator of the price volatility of a stock or other asset in comparison with the broader market. It suggests the level of risk that an investor takes on in buying the stock. The higher the beta number, the higher the risk. |
